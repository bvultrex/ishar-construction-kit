## Next implementation step

Use this exact set as the scene-reconstruction worklist. Trace the corresponding
DJ1/DJ2 script branches and composite children to recover placement, mirroring
and distance before assigning runtime wall/opening/door roles. Preserve the
corrected FDJ1 #2 and FDJ2 #1 palettes. These are scene assemblies, not automatically
repeatable wall/floor/ceiling textures. Unlisted IDs remain unclassified by this note.

## Runtime checkpoint (2026-09-26)

DJ1 47–50 / 59–62 and DJ2 35–38 / 47–50 now feed automatically selected, palette-resolved wall layers at depths 0–3. Left layers mirror the right family. This is a CK camera reconstruction supported by indexed draw expressions, not a verified original scene renderer. The other listed composites remain candidates. See HANDOFF.md for script offsets, projection assumptions and browser validation.

## Door and side-passage audit (2026-09-26)

DJ1 #73–76 combine stone arch/column artwork with four progressively smaller
scene sprites. They are now mapped to right-side passages at depths 0–3, with
mirrored copies on the left. The sprite content and dimensions are inspected;
the passage role, mirror and topology placement are a CK reconstruction. The
native sprites remain unwarped and the fallback aperture no longer adds an
artificial bright outline.

DJ1 #114–119 were tested as prospective closed-door graphics, but inspection of
their component images and a rendered fixture identified hanging chain lamps.
They are **not** door leaves. DJ2 #77–80 contain stained-glass artwork and are
likewise not generic door leaves. The inspected DJ1/DJ2 bitmap families do not
yet establish an original closed door panel. DJ1 #63–66 and DJ2 #51–54 are
open door *surrounds* only; the closed panel remains an explicit procedural
placeholder. Other module tables and scripted drawing paths need investigation
before claiming that Ishar's original door leaves have been found.

This independently supports the existing **open surround** classification for DJ2 #51. ## Closed-door visual references (2026-09-27)

At [part 31, 0:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOFb6Nt0mE8&t=10s), the original brown dungeon shows a **closed** dark, vertically planked/slatted door directly ahead, with transverse joints and an arched upper edge. The stone surround is the distinctive carved DJ1 arch: locally decoded `DJ1.IO #25` (left), `#26` (top), and `#27` (right) are children of front composite `#63`; smaller `#64–66` use corresponding near-to-far variants. This independently supports the surround classification, and establishes the *appearance* of one original closed door. It does not identify the door-leaf source pixels or the exact compositing instructions. The recording's commentary says the door is locked, which supports the closed state but not a resource ID.

At [part 22, 3:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=umlygu421CA&t=213s) and [3:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=umlygu421CA&t=220s), the grey/skull dungeon shows a **closed barred gate** at increasing apparent size. The spoken description calls it a grille door. The skull artwork matches the already identified `DJ2.IO #26` / `#51` surround family, but the barred leaf itself is not identified. The different silhouettes justify separate *procedural fallback* styles for DJ1 and DJ2 in the CK playtest; they do not justify assigning any decoded bitmap to `door.front.closed`.

The ALIS tables of `DJ1.IO` (124 entries: 66 indexed images, 58 composites) and `DJ2.IO` (118 entries: 68 indexed images, 50 composites) were fully enumerated. Their visible raw and composite previews do not contain a matching standalone closed leaf. Additional inspected modules `DJCAT`, `DJDEC`, `DJTRAP`, `DJFONT`, `MAIN`, `FRISE`, `VJAIL`, `VPORT`, `VARCHE`, `VARCADE`, `DJCOL`, and `FOND1` also yielded no match. This is a bounded negative result, **not** proof that the original game draws the leaf procedurally or that no other resource contains it. The next evidence task is tracing the closed-door draw path in the original scripts/runtime or finding the corresponding resource in the remaining modules.

## 2026-09-27 — Camera and HUD video comparison

The [part 19 corridor at 8:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hyqt3vIr7X8&t=482s) shows flat upper wall alignment and one continuous stone floor across depth. The [part 31 closed DJ1 door at 0:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOFb6Nt0mE8&t=10s) keeps the carved surround aligned with the front wall and shows a five-character action/life strip with right-hand controls. The [part 22 DJ2 opening at 3:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=umlygu421CA&t=213s) shows the grey skull surround and another right command/equipment area. Video compression and these isolated frames do not reveal exact room geometry, ALIS draw order, closed-leaf bytes or original HUD action semantics.

The CK previously used `DUNGEON_ROOM_FRAMES` with guessed sloping top values for authored rooms while corridors used `DUNGEON_NATIVE_FRAMES`. That moved ceiling, walls, arches and distant front walls at room boundaries. DJ1/DJ2 rooms now use the same native depth frames as corridors. Native FDJ floor art is drawn once behind depth layers to avoid gaps from per-depth clipping. A shifted, clipped depth-matched front wall fills the visible lateral band of an adjacent room cell. That lateral fill remains a topology-based heuristic; imported arch identity and source coordinates remain separate from the unverified CK room-composition rule.

## Side-facing room niche (2026-09-27)

In `DJ1.IO`, composite #71 contains raw #10 and #35 at X=44; composite #72 contains raw #11 and #36 at X=30. Their side-facing masonry/niche artwork occupies the same script X positions as isolated column composites #74 and #75, respectively. #71/#72 are now catalogued as room-boundary side-opening candidates at depths 1/2, while #73–76 remain generic side-opening columns. At the user's 2×2 room, #71 visually frames the right passage more plausibly than either #74 alone or the opaque #68 wall. This does not prove the original map encoding, draw order, passability rule, or that the dark backing represents a closed door leaf. The authored map edge is an open room-to-corridor connection without a door object; no blocking cell was inserted.
